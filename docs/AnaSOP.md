## 1. Research Objective

### Central Research Question

- Research question: Under extreme-rainfall scenarios in Chiyoda, Chuo and Minato, how does road impassability affect vehicle mobility and resident accessibility, and to what extent do barrier, drainage and combined measures mitigate these consequences or redistribute flood risk?
- Why it matters: Loss of a few consequential road connections can disrupt access beyond the locally flooded area. Restored passage, reduced traffic cost and protected people are different outcomes and must be evaluated separately.
- Data support currently visible: Terrain and urban geometry, five historical radar rainfall parents, numerically qualified surface-water scenarios, a directed vehicle network, survey-derived demand proxies, capacity assumptions and an official 2020 resident baseline. Registered physical measures cover two rain parents, two receiving-water assumptions and four matched arms.
- Readable concepts and current scope: Rainfall Forcing, Receiving Water Stage, Urban Geometry, Road Depth, Closed Road Length, Closed Road Duration, Vehicle Access, Unreachable Vehicle Requests, Common OD Cost, Resident Access Loss, Paired Resident Benefit and Outside Corridor Depth Increase. These are a question-facing subset of the 24 main-analysis concepts confirmed by the user. Numerical Admission remains quality control; Daytime Population and Urban Function Access remain pending constructs. Publication presentation and final claims remain open.
- What would verify the conditional question: Reproducible synchronized comparisons on common admitted support, conserved population and demand denominators, explicit unknown branches, and separate reporting of restored access, traffic cost and new losses. Greater generality would require relevant additional event coverage or observed impact evidence.
- What would weaken it: Artificial boundary isolation, unsupported road elevation, unresolved demand or capacity, unqualified population attachment, temporal mismatch, hidden adverse effects, or treating computational consistency as observed historical accuracy.
- Required feasibility check and current status: The completed review maps the central question and four supporting questions to their outcomes, units and comparison support. All five are partly testable: qualified conditional outcomes exist, while empirical impact validation and broader human-consequence claims retain explicit gaps. The subsequent preprocessing contract confirms 24 main-analysis concepts and ten lossless analysis tables; it does not resolve those empirical gaps.

### Scope of Analysis

- Geographic scope: report the three central wards and retain the adjoining hydraulic and road connections needed to avoid artificial boundary disconnection. Maintain a whole-area view; this stage does not initiate pointwise engineering inspection.
- Primary outcome scope: road impassability, sampled closure duration, conditional vehicle access, modeled traffic cost and nighttime resident-stock access loss. The broader approved aim includes daytime people and urban functions, but those outcomes remain separate unresolved components rather than silently completed analyses.
- Hazard scope: use prescribed historical radar rainfall and explicit receiving-water scenarios. Historical rain dates, scaled descendants and controls are not certified return periods or independent observed flood validations. The task remains consequence analysis rather than rainfall prediction.
- Urban geometry scope: terrain, buildings, roads and surface connectivity derived from three-dimensional city information constrain the two-dimensional surface-water calculation. Their value is tested by matched representation contrasts; complete three-dimensional water flow, river bathymetry, sewer hydraulics and underground tunnels are not represented.
- Transport scope: motor vehicles are primary. Walking, station, subway and JR layers remain complementary scenarios and possible substitutes, not implicitly available or implicitly failed modes. Conditional loss of vehicle access to designated external portals does not establish complete multimodal isolation of an entire district.
- Coverage scope: unknown road exposure and unresolved normal population attachment remain separate pools. Coverage fractions are reported with their actual length and stock denominators; they are not extrapolation factors for the rest of the city.
- Population scope: preserve separate nighttime and daytime accounting. The current official resident baseline and uncertain building allocations do not establish event-time occupancy, actual evacuation, return-home failure or unique affected-person counts. Larger candidate radii do not automatically improve supported coverage.
- Urban-function scope: future claims require declared destinations, operating states, capacity and an access criterion. A flooded nearby road, building use label or portal-disconnected origin alone does not demonstrate actual service loss.
- Time scope: compare simultaneous states within the registered nine-hour dry-start windows. Five-minute fields support sampled trajectories; omitted antecedents and unobserved post-window recovery remain limitations. A cellwise maximum-depth envelope is not a simultaneous citywide closure state.
- Intervention scope: keep idealized road reopening, demand/guidance assumptions and physically simulated barriers/pumps distinct. Physical placement and capacity remain frozen within the completed comparison; no equal-cost, optimal-design or surveyed-effectiveness claim follows.
- Generalization scope: the five historical rain parents support a small conditional event comparison. The physical-measure block covers two parents; stages, frames and measures do not expand the independent event count. The selection-event and transfer contexts remain labeled.

## 2. Theoretical Background  /  Conceptual Framework  /  Problem Formulation

### Research Gap

The applied gap is the connection between surface inundation, road operations, network access and human consequences, together with the possibility that interventions improve one outcome while worsening another. Flooded area alone does not identify inaccessible routes, demand-dependent cost, population-access loss or urban-function failure. The present evidence supports several conditional links in this chain while leaving actual daytime presence, service continuity and observed congestion incompletely established. This is a statement of the project's evidence needs, not a reviewed literature-novelty claim.

### Conceptual Framework

Prescribed Rainfall Forcing and Receiving Water Stage interact with Terrain and Urban Geometry to produce simultaneous Surface Water State. Road Depth changes Closed Road Length and Slow Road Length. Vehicle Network, Vehicle Demand, Road Capacity and Reference Travel Cost determine conditional Vehicle Access, Unreachable Vehicle Requests and Common OD Cost. Resident Population and Population Access Candidates then bound Resident Access Loss. Broader daytime and function-specific consequences require additional exposure and service evidence.

Three distinct mechanisms organize the study. First, local water depth removes or degrades road passage. Second, those changes redistribute represented trips and may change costs even where a path remains. Third, loss of feasible access candidates changes which resident stocks can retain their normal vehicle connection. A physical barrier or drainage action modifies the hydraulic input to these mechanisms; traffic guidance or deferral acts through a different operational channel.

Potential benefits are not assumed to align. A restored connection can admit additional trips while costs for a fixed surviving cohort remain higher. A barrier can retain or redirect surface water and create adverse locations outside a protected corridor. A pump's source-area benefit does not establish that the receiving river has adequate storage or outlet capacity. The study compares these separate outcomes and retains negative, zero and positive effects.

Urban geometry is an explanatory representation within the hydraulic consequence chain. A difference between terrain-only and urban-constrained results establishes information sensitivity; improved real-world accuracy would require appropriate validation. Similarly, the existence of uncertain exposure does not erase qualified conditional results, but it limits extrapolation and the interpretation of transport islands.

### Problem Formulation

The model-comparison unit combines a rain parent, prescribed stage, geometry or measure configuration, actual output time and declared spatial support. Roads and native hydraulic cells are physical/network units; official statistical units supply resident stock; origin-destination requests supply represented traffic demand. Spatial cells, time frames, stage variants, geometry variants and intervention arms remain dependent descendants of their source event and configuration.

Use matched forcing, initial state, solver version, grid, source volumes and admitted support to isolate a model contrast. Geometry comparisons, five-parent no-action consequence analysis and the two-parent physical-measure comparison are distinct experimental blocks; they do not form an unexecuted complete factorial. Whole-window road summaries and synchronized fixed-time traffic/population comparisons address different outcome definitions and retain their own denominators.

The interpretable results are coverage-qualified road interruption, conditional mode-specific reachability, restricted modeled cost, resident-stock access intervals and paired intervention changes with adverse transfer retained. They are not observational causal policy effects, complete multimodal isolation, actual stranded-person counts or safety certification. The completed feasibility review distinguishes absent constructs from missing records in existing variables. Confirmed analysis concepts retain their frozen quality support, paired uncertainty bounds and unresolved pools; pending daytime and function-specific outcomes are not manufactured to complete the question set.

### Geometry and receiving-water contrasts

\[
 H_{k,g}=\max_t h_{k,g}(t),\qquad A_g(\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{G}}a_k\,\mathbf{1}\{H_{k,g}\geq\theta\},\qquad \delta H_k=H_{k,\mathrm{urban}}-H_{k,\mathrm{terrain}}.
\]

Here \(k\) is a matched spatial sample, \(t\) is actual elapsed time in seconds, \(\mathbf{1}\) is the indicator, and \(g\) is the terrain-only or urban-constrained geometry, \(h_{k,g}(t)\) is modeled surface depth on the stated original bound branch, \(H_{k,g}\) is its sampled window maximum, \(\mathcal{G}\) is the original common reporting support, \(a_k\) is the original clipped area weight in square km, \(\theta\) is an already registered wetness threshold, \(A_g(\theta)\) is threshold-exceeding peak-envelope area and \(\delta H_k\) is the difference between cellwise peak envelopes. The subscripts \(\mathrm{urban}\) and \(\mathrm{terrain}\) name the two representations. The maximum operator uses the existing registered window and output times.

Use the original area weights and censor bounds rather than substituting uniform 10 m weights for the earlier fine-support geometry summaries. Main existing contrasts use 0.10 m for open-ground wet area and 0.30 m for classified-road wet area. Wet area is not Closed Road Length, and a cellwise peak envelope is not simultaneous flooding. Keep no-rain, reference-rain and scaled-rain contrasts separate by Receiving Water Stage. Nonlinear contrasts are not additive rainfall-versus-river source fractions. This specification feeds Geometry and Water-Level Sensitivity and the scope table.

### Road consequence metrics

\[
 L_m(t)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{Q}} l_e\,\mathbf{1}\{d_{e,m}(t)\geq 0.30\},\qquad K_m=\frac{1}{3600}\int_0^T L_m(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\]

Here \(m\) is a measure arm, \(\mathcal{Q}\) is the common qualified road set, \(e\) is a road, \(l_e\) is unique ward-clipped length in km, \(d_{e,m}(t)\) is the mapped depth in meters on a stated lower/upper branch, \(L_m(t)\) is closed length in km, \(T=32400\) s is the window endpoint and \(K_m\) is sampled closed-road exposure in km h. Each source geometry is union-clipped before three-ward totals; separate ward and protected/other-road strata are retained.

\[
 \Delta L_m=\max_t L_0(t)-\max_t L_m(t),\qquad \Delta K_m=K_0-K_m.
\]

Here subscript \(0\) denotes no measure, \(\Delta L_m\) is peak-to-peak avoided closed length and \(\Delta K_m\) is avoided closed-road exposure. Peaks need not coincide. Positive reductions indicate benefit; negative reductions indicate deterioration. Percent changes are undefined when the baseline is zero. Sampled closure duration does not establish exact onset/recovery or full drainage after nine hours.

\[
 S_m(t)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{Q}}l_e\,\mathbf{1}\{0.10\leq d_{e,m}(t)<0.30\},\qquad K_m\approx\frac{1}{3600}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{L_m(t_j)+L_m(t_{j+1})}{2}(t_{j+1}-t_j).
\]

Here \(S_m(t)\) is Slow Road Length in km, \(j\) indexes consecutive output intervals, \(J=108\) is the interval count and \(t_j\) is an approved analysis time in seconds. The road quantities and support retain the definitions above. This is the existing trapezoidal length-time exposure, not a count of distinct closed roads or an exact physical closure duration. ### Vehicle access before population attachment

\[
 \ell_{v,m}(t)=\chi_v^{\mathrm{normal}}[1-\chi_{v,m}(t)].
\]

Here \(v\) is an existing network candidate node, \(\chi_v^{\mathrm{normal}}\) indicates its qualified normal bidirectional portal access, \(\chi_{v,m}(t)\) indicates that both an outward and an inward permitted directed path remain at the simultaneous road state, and \(\ell_{v,m}(t)\) is loss of normal Vehicle Access. ### Resident access and paired uncertainty

\[
 N_m^{L}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}P_i\min_{v\in S_i}\ell_{v,m}(t),\qquad N_m^{U}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}P_i\max_{v\in S_i}\ell_{v,m}(t).
\]

Here \(\mathcal{I}\) is the complete-baseline attachment set of statistical units, \(i\) is one unit, \(P_i\) is its official 2020 resident stock, \(S_i\) is its frozen access-candidate set, and \(N_m^{L}(t),N_m^{U}(t)\) are all-candidate and any-candidate loss bounds.
